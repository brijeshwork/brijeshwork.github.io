📋 Overview

Executive Summary

SMS (Social Media Scheduler) is a multi-tenant SaaS platform solving the operational bottleneck of manual social media publishing for creators and agencies. This document covers the full product lifecycle: from problem discovery and user research through system architecture, API design, and delivery.

Purpose

Enable creators and agencies to manage every stage of their content lifecycle — from idea to publication to analytics — without manual intervention. SMS removes the need to publish content manually, remember posting schedules, or switch between multiple tools.

AI writes the caption. You pick the time. The queue does the rest. No more scheduling marathons every Sunday night.

😴

📊

Know exactly which content earns you followers

🧭 Philosophy

Product Principles

These are the non-negotiable rules that guide every feature decision, scope cut, and trade-off in this product. When in doubt, we come back to these.

P1
Reliability over richness
A tool that works perfectly beats a tool that does everything poorly.
Product Example

We shipped to one platform (Instagram) with a 99%+ publish SLA rather than 5 platforms with a 90% rate. Users reported zero missed posts in Week 1.

Future Influence

Every new platform integration must pass a stability gate — a 99%+ publish success rate in staging — before it ships to users.

P2
Solve the core job first
No feature earns a place until the fundamental loop is proven to work.
Product Example

Team Collaboration and Best Time to Post were excluded from MVP — not because they're unimportant, but because neither is useful if the core "Schedule → Publish → Confirm" loop isn't rock-solid first.

Future Influence

Phase 2 and beyond are unlocked only after we validate D30 retention. We don't build on top of an unproven foundation.

P3
Eliminate anxiety, don't just reduce it
Every user has a moment of doubt before committing. Design removes it completely.
Product Example

We rejected the "Click to Preview" modal — it reduced anxiety but reintroduced friction. The side-by-side live preview eliminates it entirely: users see the exact output as they type.

Future Influence

Every new screen goes through a "where is the anxiety?" audit before design begins. We ask: what does the user fear at this moment — and how do we remove the fear entirely?

P4
Data gates decisions, not opinions
We don't move to the next phase until specific metrics say we're ready.
Product Example

Phase 3 (Best Time to Post AI) is gated on 90 days of engagement data from 500+ active accounts. We start the sprint when the data is ready — not when the calendar says Q3.

Future Influence

Every roadmap item has a documented trigger metric. Scope changes are approved when a number is crossed, not in a quarterly planning meeting.

P5
Architecture earns trust later
Build the right foundations now so tomorrow's features take weeks, not months.
Product Example

The publishing worker is built as a platform-agnostic abstraction. Adding LinkedIn in Phase 2 requires one new API adapter, not a system rewrite. We paid the engineering cost in Week 1 so Phase 2 takes weeks, not months.

Future Influence

Before any new feature enters the sprint, engineering signs off on whether the foundation can absorb it without structural debt. If it can't, the foundation gets fixed first.

P6
Deferred is documented, not forgotten
Every excluded feature has a reason on record and a condition that brings it back.
Product Example

Team Collaboration is documented as "excluded because MVP persona doesn't need it yet — revisit when Agency plan exceeds 30% of MRR." Not dropped. Deferred with a clear return condition.

Future Influence

The roadmap doc is a living record of deferred decisions and their triggers — not a wishlist. Stakeholders can always see why something isn't in the current sprint and exactly what would change that.

P7
Outcomes over outputs
We don't ship features. We ship improvements to how users feel, earn, or save time.
Product Example

We didn't ship "AI Caption Generation." We shipped the ability to go from a blank page to a publish-ready caption in 90 seconds — reducing post-creation time by 93%. The feature is the implementation; the outcome is the story.

Future Influence

Every sprint ticket has an "outcome statement" alongside the feature description. Engineers and designers know the user goal, not just the spec. A feature that ships without moving its target metric is treated as a miss, not a win.

The Test

Before shipping anything, we ask: "If this feature worked perfectly but users didn't feel any different — would we still ship it?" If the answer is yes, we have built an output. If no, we have built an outcome.

Strategic Decision Architecture

Launching an Omnichannel MVP

Why we deprioritized a multi-platform launch in favor of a single unified architecture.

✓ The Problem

Agencies manage fragmented audiences across LinkedIn, X, Pinterest, and Facebook. However, building 5 distinct OAuth connections and queue engines simultaneously would take 4 months, delaying the core promise: reliable auto-publishing.

✓ Options Considered

⊘ Launch All Platforms
Delay launch by 12 weeks to build 5 integrations.
✓ Unified Architecture First
Build one robust queue engine, add platforms later.

✓ Final Decision

We chose to launch a unified architecture focusing purely on the most mature Graph API first.

✓ Why This Option Won

Users explicitly told us that a buggy multi-platform tool is far worse than a rock-solid single platform tool. We prioritized the stability of the Queue Engine over the breadth of platforms.

✓ Tradeoffs

  • Lost Early Adopters: Agencies requiring an immediate omnichannel solution (X + LinkedIn) churned during onboarding.
  • UI Complexity: Building a modular monolith meant over-engineering the internal architecture for a single platform.
📈
✓ Business Impact

Shipped a highly stable v1 in 6 weeks with zero dropped posts in week 1. Validated product-market fit rapidly, allowing us to build X/LinkedIn in Phase 2 in 1/3 the time.

Product Assumptions

Every product decision is built on a set of beliefs. These are the critical assumptions we are betting the MVP on — and how we will know if we are wrong.

A1
Creators will pay for reliable auto-publishing over manual posting.
The core value proposition assumption
🔶 High Risk
Why We Believe It

Manual posting requires constant presence. Creators report spending 2+ hours/day on scheduling logistics alone — time they would redirect to content creation if automation existed.

Evidence

Buffer and Hootsuite collectively serve 20M+ users at $15–$99/mo. 18/25 creators in discovery interviews said they'd switch tools for a more reliable queue engine.

Risk If Wrong

Users treat scheduling as a free utility and refuse to upgrade from Free tier. MRR stalls and the Pro conversion rate stays below 2%, making the SaaS model unviable.

Validation Plan

Monitor Free → Pro conversion rate in weeks 2–6. If below 3% with >100 active users, trigger a pricing and value-prop interview sprint within 2 weeks.

A2
AI-generated captions will drive activation — not just delight.
The differentiation assumption
🔶 High Risk
Why We Believe It

The blank caption field is the #1 drop-off point in our scheduler prototype. AI captions eliminate the blank canvas anxiety and reduce post creation time from ~8 minutes to ~90 seconds.

Evidence

In prototype testing, 9/10 users who used AI caption generation completed their first scheduled post. Only 5/10 users who skipped it did. Completion rate doubled with the feature active.

Risk If Wrong

Users try the feature once and revert to manual captions, treating it as a gimmick. AI adoption stays below 20% and doesn't drive the expected lift in activation or Week-1 retention.

Validation Plan

Track AI caption adoption rate weekly. If below 40% after 200 active users, redesign the onboarding prompt and retest. Compare D7 retention for AI users vs. non-AI users as the true signal.

A3
A single-platform MVP is sufficient to validate product-market fit.
The scope assumption
🟢 Moderate Risk
Why We Believe It

The core scheduling problem is platform-agnostic. If we can't achieve retention on a single network, adding more platforms won't fix a flawed core loop — it will only mask it briefly.

Evidence

Instagram is the #1 requested platform in user interviews (22/25 respondents). It has the most mature Graph API for content publishing, reducing integration risk significantly vs. X/TikTok.

Risk If Wrong

Target agencies require LinkedIn or X as primary publishing channels and churn during onboarding because we don't support them yet, blocking our path to agency plan revenue.

Validation Plan

Tag every churn reason at offboarding. If "missing platform" accounts for >25% of churns in Month 1, fast-track LinkedIn integration and move Phase 2 timeline forward by 6 weeks.

A4
A <10 minute onboarding time is achievable without a product tour.
The activation assumption
🟢 Moderate Risk
Why We Believe It

The OAuth → Create Post → Schedule flow has only 3 steps. If the UI is self-explanatory, an empty state CTA is more effective than a 6-step walkthrough that users skip.

Evidence

In moderated prototype tests, users who received no instructions completed the OAuth → first scheduled post flow in an average of 7 minutes 42 seconds without assistance.

Risk If Wrong

OAuth reconnect confusion causes high early churn. Users abandon during account connection and never experience the core value, skewing all activation and retention data.

Validation Plan

Instrument the onboarding funnel with step-by-step drop-off tracking. If OAuth drop-off exceeds 30%, ship an inline contextual guide at that specific step — not a full product tour.

A5
A queue-based architecture will maintain 99%+ publish success rate at MVP scale.
The reliability assumption
🟣 Technical Risk
Why We Believe It

Redis-backed job queues are a well-understood pattern for timed delivery. The risk lies in API rate limits and token expiry — both are manageable with retry logic and proactive token refresh.

Evidence

Internal load testing at 500 concurrent scheduled jobs showed 0 failures with the retry mechanism active. Instagram Graph API rate limits are well-documented and predictable at MVP user volumes.

Risk If Wrong

Silent publish failures at scale destroy user trust irreversibly. A batch of missed scheduled posts for an agency during a campaign launch could result in mass churn and reputational damage.

Validation Plan

Monitor publish success rate via ops dashboard daily in Weeks 1–4. Any dip below 98.5% triggers an immediate engineering review. Push notification alerts ship with v1 as a trust signal.

The Two-Panel Live Preview

Eliminating "Post-and-Delete" anxiety through real-time native rendering.

✓ The Problem

7/10 users expressed severe anxiety before clicking "Schedule". Single-panel schedulers force users to mentally simulate how line breaks and images will look natively, causing a 25% drop-off rate.

✓ Options Considered

⊘ Modal Previews
A "Click to Preview" button that launches a popup overlay.
✓ Side-by-Side Panel
A 65% input / 35% live-preview layout.

✓ Final Decision

We shifted to a Side-by-Side Live Rendering layout. As the user types, the right pane instantly renders a pixel-perfect mockup.

✓ Why This Option Won

Modal previews solved the visual anxiety but disrupted the user's flow. Users had to repeatedly close the modal to edit typos. Side-by-side provides a continuous, frictionless validation loop.

✓ Tradeoffs

  • Maintenance Overhead: Requires constant updates to our CSS if native social networks change their layouts.
  • Screen Real Estate: The layout feels cramped on 13" laptops, requiring complex responsive breakpoints.
📈
✓ Business Impact

Reduced post-creation abandonment by 40%. Built instant user trust, making the platform feel "premium" and responsive.

MVP Scope & Product Roadmap

Every item in or out of the MVP has a documented reason. We don't defer features because of time — we defer them because the data to make them well doesn't exist yet.

What ships in MVP — and why

These 5 capabilities were selected because removing any one of them makes the product non-viable.

6 Weeks · v1.0
IN
Social OAuth Connect — One-click account linking
Foundation
👤 Customer Reasoning

Without account connection, the product is literally empty. Users can't schedule, preview, or publish anything. It's not a feature — it's the prerequisite for every other user action.

💼 Business Reasoning

Our entire value proposition is automatic publishing. Without OAuth, we cannot fulfil a single unit of that promise. This is the gateway to activation, retention, and ultimately revenue.

⚙️ Engineering Reasoning

Instagram's Graph API is the most mature and well-documented of all major social platforms. Building the OAuth token lifecycle (connect, refresh, revoke) here sets the pattern for every platform we add in Phase 2.

IN
Queue-Based Auto Publishing — Posts go live on time, automatically
Core Loop
👤 Customer Reasoning

This is the product. The entire reason a creator switches tools is to stop manually posting. If a scheduled post doesn't go out automatically, we've failed the core contract and they will leave.

💼 Business Reasoning

Our 99%+ publish success rate is the primary metric behind Pro plan upgrades. Agencies won't pay without this SLA. Every missed post is a churn event before we've had a chance to demonstrate value.

⚙️ Engineering Reasoning

Redis-backed job queues with exponential backoff and idempotency keys are the right architecture for this problem. Building this correctly once creates the reliable backbone that every future platform integration will use.

IN
AI Caption Generation — A publish-ready caption in 90 seconds
Differentiator
👤 Customer Reasoning

The blank caption field is the #1 drop-off point. Creators spend 30–40 minutes per caption — time they don't have. The "aha moment" in prototype testing happened in 90 seconds when users saw a ready-to-post caption generated from their image.

💼 Business Reasoning

This is what separates us from Buffer and Hootsuite. AI captions are our primary acquisition hook and the feature most cited in user interviews as the reason they'd switch from an existing tool. It drives activation, not just delight.

⚙️ Engineering Reasoning

Using the OpenAI API via a thin service layer keeps this stateless and cost-per-use. There is no training data dependency in v1 — we call the API, stream the output. Simple, fast, proven, and replaceable if a better model emerges.

IN
Media Library — All your content assets, organised in one place
Workflow
👤 Customer Reasoning

90%+ of posts include a visual. Without a media library, users must re-upload the same assets every time. For agencies managing brand assets across clients, this is a hard blocker — not an inconvenience.

💼 Business Reasoning

Agencies evaluating tools immediately check "where do my assets live?" A media library is table stakes for the Agency plan. Without it, we can't compete for the $49/mo tier that funds long-term growth.

⚙️ Engineering Reasoning

S3-compatible object storage with per-account namespacing is the correct pattern. Building this in v1 avoids migrating file storage later — a notoriously disruptive operation in production systems.

IN
Retry Failed Jobs — When a post fails, we fix it automatically before you notice
Trust
👤 Customer Reasoning

A single missed post is a trust-destroying event — especially for an agency that promised a client their campaign would go live at 9am. Users won't give us a second chance after a silent failure during a critical window.

💼 Business Reasoning

Without automated retries, we can't offer any SLA. Without an SLA, we can't charge Pro or Agency prices. Retry logic and failure alerting are what transform a "scheduling tool" into a "publishing platform" that justifies a subscription.

⚙️ Engineering Reasoning

Exponential backoff (1m → 5m → 15m) with a 3-attempt max prevents hammering a rate-limited API while still recovering from transient failures. Idempotency keys ensure no duplicate posts. Failure state is persisted for audit logging.

What was deliberately excluded — and when it gets built

Every deferred item has a documented trigger. We don't revisit these until the data says the moment is right.

PHASE 2
Publish to every platform from a single queue — LinkedIn, X, Facebook
Post-MVP · ~Q3
Why Excluded from MVP

Building 4 additional OAuth flows, rate limit systems, and media format handlers simultaneously would add 12 weeks to the MVP. We'd ship nothing well in an attempt to ship everything. A stable v1 on one platform generates more trust than an unstable v0.5 on five.

Why the Architecture Is Already Ready

The queue engine, OAuth token lifecycle, and publishing worker are built as platform-agnostic abstractions. Adding a new platform in Phase 2 means writing one new API adapter — not rearchitecting the system. The modular monolith was designed for this exact moment.

🔔 Build Trigger

Start when "missing platform" accounts for >25% of churn reasons OR when NPS exceeds 50 and we have a stable D30 retention baseline. Whichever comes first.

PHASE 3
Know the exact moment your audience is most likely to engage — AI Best Time to Post
Post-Phase 2 · ~Q4
Why Excluded from MVP

Meaningful posting-time recommendations require at minimum 3 months of per-account engagement history. Building the model before the training data exists produces noise, not insight — and damages user trust when recommendations underperform.

Why It Matters Enough to Plan

Timing is the single biggest lever on content performance for creators. A tool that tells you when your audience is online, not just how to schedule — is the step from "useful" to "irreplaceable." This is the Phase 3 retention hook.

🔔 Build Trigger

Start when we have a minimum of 90 days of engagement data from at least 500 active accounts. Data quality is the gate — not calendar time.

PHASE 4
Let your whole team schedule content without stepping on each other — Team Collaboration
Future · ~H1 2027
Why Excluded from MVP

Multi-user workspaces, role-based access control, and approval workflows add 4–6 weeks of complexity. Our MVP persona — the solo creator and small agency owner — doesn't need this yet. We must prove the core solo loop before building a layer on top of it.

Why It Unlocks a New Tier

Team collaboration is the gateway to the enterprise and mid-market agency segment — contracts of $200–$500/mo per workspace. This is not a feature addition; it's an entirely new revenue tier that changes our go-to-market strategy.

🔔 Build Trigger

Start when Agency plan accounts represent >30% of MRR, or when 3+ enterprise prospects cite "no team access" as the primary blocker to signing. The market will tell us when it's time.

Glossary

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What happens if a user's Social OAuth token expires while posts are scheduled?
A: The background worker will detect the invalid token, mark the job as Failed, and send an immediate email/in-app notification to the user to reconnect their account.

Q: How are daylight saving time (DST) changes handled?
A: Since we store publish times in UTC and use the server's IANA timezone library to convert from the user's selected timezone, DST shifts are handled automatically without user intervention.

Q: Can users publish Carousels?
A: Yes. The UI allows uploading up to 10 images/videos per post. The publishing worker uses the Platform APIs's carousel endpoints to group the media.
